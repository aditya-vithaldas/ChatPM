Commerce / Discovery / Concept outline

E-commerce discovery concept

Helping people move from a broad shopping intent to a useful shortlist.

Try the concept ↓

01 / The question

How might discovery help shoppers articulate what they need, rather than make them navigate an overwhelming catalog?

The following is an initial concept hypothesis, not a report of completed research or measured results.

02 / Thought process

The why.

Begin with the shopping mission: what someone is trying to find, compare, or decide.

The what.

A focused discovery experience that combines browsing, simple filters, and a saved shortlist.

The how.

Test a small catalog and a few meaningful filters before introducing recommendations or complex personalization.

03 / Try the interaction

A small part of the experience.

Choose a category, search the sample catalog, then save a few items to a shortlist.

Find your everyday favorites.

0 saved

Interactive sample only. Changes stay in this page and reset when reloaded. No real customer data or external systems are connected.

04 / Design rationale

The decision behind the interaction.

Make narrowing the choice feel lightweight. Keeping the shortlist close to discovery lets someone compare without losing their place.

05 / What to learn next

From hypothesis to evidence.

Observe whether shoppers can find relevant options and explain why they saved them. Learn which filters reflect real intent.
